What a simulated truth can and cannot show

The bundled examples are generated by a simulator that keeps the truth for every event: its cell type, its fluorochrome abundances, whether it falls inside a clog or bubble, and the true spillover, phase fractions, precursor frequencies and batch effects. That tests the software against answers known exactly, which real data cannot provide. It does not test whether the simulator is realistic. The simulator models what makes real analyses hard: spillover and spreading from photon statistics, lymphoid and myeloid autofluorescence, dead cells, debris and doublets, clogs, bubbles and drift, and batch effects. Real data can still fail in ways it does not model, which is why the suite also runs on real instrument files, real FlowJo workspaces and the R packages these methods come from.

Methods it reimplements

CytoWeave's analyses are reimplementations of published methods. Their authors are cited in the code and in the methods paragraph CytoWeave writes for your analysis, among them:

  • Logicle: Parks, Roederer and Moore
  • PeacoQC: Emmaneel et al.
  • FlowSOM and CytoNorm: Van Gassen et al.
  • UMAP: McInnes et al.; t-SNE: van der Maaten and Hinton
  • Leiden: Traag et al.; Louvain: Blondel et al.
  • k-means++: Arthur and Vassilvitskii; Hamerly's k-means
  • The spillover spreading matrix: Nguyen et al.
  • Marker enrichment modeling (MEM): Diggins et al.
  • Trustworthiness and continuity: Venna and Kaski; LISI: Korsunsky et al.
  • MIFlowCyt, FCS and Gating-ML: the International Society for Advancement of Cytometry (ISAC)

The Report view lists the references for the analyses you actually used, with DOIs and a BibTeX file; see the methods paragraph.

Cite CytoWeave

If you use CytoWeave in published work, please cite it together with the methods listed in its Report view. The repository's CITATION.cff holds the citation, and GitHub's Cite this repository button formats it.

bibtex
@software{cytoweave,
  author  = {McDermott, Robert},
  title   = {CytoWeave: a flow cytometry analysis workbench},
  version = {{{version}}},
  url     = {https://github.com/robert-mcdermott/cytoweave},
  license = {Apache-2.0},
  year    = {2026}
}

Privacy and security

  • Files are parsed and analyzed in the browser. Nothing is uploaded, and CytoWeave makes no network requests of its own.
  • By default the server listens only on the loopback interface, rejects requests whose Host header is not a local address (which blocks DNS rebinding), accepts API calls only from its own pages, and serves a strict Content Security Policy.
  • Remote control is off unless --remote-control is given (cytoweave mcp turns it on for its own session). Actions that read files from disk need a per-session token.

Research software. CytoWeave is not a medical device and must not be used for diagnosis.
